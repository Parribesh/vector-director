## What you can use
- Who can speak, in `who`: {{speakers}}. "narrator" tells the story and isn't in the scene. Those who aren't on screen
  are only heard.
- People on screen here: {{here}}. They stand at `start` marks and move only by walking to a mark.
- Marks: {{marks}}. off-left and off-right are out of sight, for arriving or leaving.
- Poses, which stay until changed: {{poses}}. Choose the one that fits what is being said, and change often.
- Camera, per shot: wide (the whole place), two (both people), medium and close (`on` one person), over (over one
  person's shoulder onto `on`). A one-person scene uses wide, medium and close.
- portal: open a ring of light, rift (the same ring turned dark and dangerous), close, or none. It stands where the set says.
- light: dim, dark, normal, or none for no change.
- sound: wind-up, wind-down, rumble, or none.

## Rules
- Dialogue and narration in {{language}}: natural spoken language, short sentences, easy to voice. Keep names and
  technical words as people say them.
- 2 to 6 shots, each with 1 to 4 beats. Cut to a new shot when the speaker changes or the feeling turns. A good scene
  starts wide, moves in as it gets intense, and doesn't stay on one shot long.
- A beat is one spoken line, or something happening with say left empty: then who is "nobody" and it holds for a moment.
- A beat with a walk has say empty. The walker walks during it and the next beat follows when they've arrived.
- Keep the story's events and spirit, but say them through dialogue, and expand so that it plays naturally. Use only
  what the lists above allow: nobody holds, touches, sits or fights.
{{rules}}
- Don't end on a summary. End on a line that leads to the next scene.
